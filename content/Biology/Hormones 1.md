Hormone receptors are located in different parts of the cell. 
**Cell membrane:** Receptor of protein hormones and adrenal medullary hormones (catecholamines) have receptor in cell membrane
**Cytoplasm** : Steroid hormones
**Neucleus**: Thyroid hormone

Hormone in the form of hormone-receptor complex enters the target cell by means of endocytosis and executes the actions. The whole process is called **internalization.**


### Sequence of Events during Activation of Genes
i. Hormone enters the interior of cell and binds with receptor in cytoplasm (steroid hormone) or in nucleus (thyroid hormone) and forms hormone-receptor complex
ii. Hormone-receptor complex moves towards the DNA and binds with DNA
iii. This increases transcription of mRNA
iv. The mRNA moves out of nucleus and reaches ribosomes and activates them
v. Activated ribosomes produce large quantities of proteins
vi. These proteins produce physiological responses in the target cells.
## Pituitary Gland
Location: a depression called sella turcica present in the sphenoid bone at the base of the skull.Connected to the hypothalamus by pituitary stalk or hypophyseal stalk.

Anterior pituitary or adenohypophysis
posterior pituitary or neurohypophysis.

between them there is a small relatively avascular structure called **Pars intermedia**. which is a part of adenohypophysis. 

### Anterior Pituitary or Adenohypophysis

- **ectodermal** in origin, arises from **pharyngeal epithelium** as an upward growth known as **Rathke Pouch**. 
- Known as **Master gland** because it regulates more other endocrine glands.
- It has three parts - 
	-  Pars distalis
	- Pars tuberalis
	- Pars intermedia
- Histology - Two types of cells, **Chromophobe cells and Chromophil cells**
- Hormones - 
	- Growth Hormone (GH), or Somatotropic hormone. 
	- Thyroid Stimulating Hormone (TSH)
	- Adrenocorticotropic hormone (ADH)
	- Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH)
	- Prolactin
	- Leuteinizing hormone (females) or Interstitial cell stimulating hormone (male)
- All hormones other than prolactin stimulates other adrenal glands, that's why they are called **Tropic Hormones**.

### Posterior Pituitary or Neurohypophysis

- **Neuroectodermal** in origin, arises from **hypothalamus** as a downward diverticulum. 
- Rathke pouch and downward diverticulum meets together. 
- **Histology:** Posterior pituitary is made up of neural type of cells called pituicytes and unmyelinated nerve fibers.
- **Pituicytes** - Pituicytes are the fusiform cells derived from glial cells. These cells have several processes and brown pigment granules. Pituicytes act as supporting cells and do not secrete any hormone.
- **Unmylinated nerve fibres** : Unmyelinated nerve fibers come from supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei of the hypothalamus through the pituitary stalk.
- **Hormones**: ADH and Oxytocin
- Actually posterior pituitary does not secrete any hormones. ADH and Oxytocin is synthesized in hypothalamus and they are transported and stored in the nerve endings of posterior pituitary. When impulses reach from hypothalamus to posterior pituitary these hormones gets released. 
- **Neurophysins** : Neurophysins are the binding proteins which transport ADH and oxytocin from hypothalamus to posterior pituitary via hypothalamo-hypophyseal tract and storage of these hormones in posterior pituitary. Neurophysin I or oxytocin-neurophysin is the binding protein for oxytocin and neurophysin II or ADH-neurophysin is the binding protein for ADH.




## Hormones and their functions 

### FSH
In males, FSH acts along with testosterone and accelerates the process of spermeogenesis (refer
Chapter 74 for details).
In females FSH:
1. Causes the development of graafian follicle from primordial follicle
2. Stimulates the theca cells of graafian follicle and causes secretion of estrogen (refer Chapter 79 for details)
3. Promotes the aromatase activity in granulosa cells, resulting in conversion of androgens into estrogen (Chapter 80).

### LH
In males, LH is known as interstitial cell-stimulating hormone (ICSH) because it stimulates the interstitial cells of Leydig in testes. This hormone is essential for the secretion of testosterone from Leydig cells (Chapter 74). 
In females, LH:
1. Causes maturation of vesicular follicle into graafian follicle along with follicle-stimulating hormone
2. Induces synthesis of androgens from theca cells of growing follicle
3. Is responsible for ovulation
4. Is necessary for the formation of corpus luteum
5. Activates the secretory functions of corpus luteum.

### GH
- Degraded in liver and kidney.
- Function: 
	- GH increases the synthesis of proteins, mobilization of lipids and conservation of carbohydrates.
	- In embryonic stage, GH is responsible for the differentiation and development of bone cells. In later stages, GH increases the growth of the skeleton. It increases both the length as well as the thickness of the bones.

### Antidiuretic Hormone 
- Secreted by **supraoptic** nucleus of hypothalamus. Also secreted by **paraventricular** nucleus of hypothalamus in a small quantity. 
- **Functions:** 
	- Retention of water - It acts on kidneys and increases facultative reabsorption in distal convoluted tubule and collecting duct. 
	- Vasopressor action - large amount of ADH shows vasoconstrictor action. It causes constriction of arteries which leads to higher blood pressure. 

### Oxytocin
- Secreted by *paraventricular nucleus* of hypothalamus. Also secreted by *supraoptic nucleus* in small quantity. 
- Function in females: 
	- **On mammary glands**: causes constriction of myo-epithelial cells, which leads to the flow of milk from alveoli of mammary glands to the outside of the body through duct system and nipple. This process is called milk ejection reflex or milk let down reflex. It is one of the **Neuroendocrine reflexes**. 
	- **Milk ejection reflex**: There are many touch receptors around the nipple, impulses from these receptors reaches the paraventricular and supraoptic nucleus of hypothalamus. Hypothalamus sends these impulses to posterior pituitary which leads to the release of oxytocin. Large amount of oxytocin is released through positive feedback mechanism. 
	- **On pregnant uterus**: Throughout the period of pregnancy release of oxytocin is inhibited by estrogen and progesterone. At the end of pregnancy when estrogen and progesterone decreases suddenly oxytocin increases. Oxytocin causes contractions of uterus. Oxytocin level increases during labor.
