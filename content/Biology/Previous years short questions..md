### What is wallace line?
The **Wallace line** or **Wallace's line** is a faunal boundary line drawn in 1859 by the British naturalist [Alfred Russel Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Russel_Wallace "Alfred Russel Wallace") and named by the English biologist [T.H. Huxley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Henry_Huxley "Thomas Henry Huxley") that separates the [biogeographical realms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biogeographical_realms "Biogeographical realms") of [Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia "Asia") and '[Wallacea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallacea "Wallacea")', a transitional zone between Asia and [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_(continent) "Australia (continent)") also called the [Malay Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Archipelago "Malay Archipelago") and the Indo-Australian Archipelago. To the west of the line are found organisms related to Asiatic species; to the east, a mixture of species of Asian and Australian origins is present. Wallace noticed this clear division in both land mammals and birds during his travels through the [East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Indies "East Indies") in the 19th century.

### Why the fibres of Rhinoderos horn are not true horn?
Keratin-fibre horn is found only in the Rhinoceros. It is a hard conical structure located on the frontonasal region of the skull, It is composed of a cluster of long fibers cemented together by a mass of hard and keratinised cells growing from the epidermis. Each fibre resembles a very thick hair and emerges from a dermal pailla. The fibres are not true hairs since their bases lack follicles.
